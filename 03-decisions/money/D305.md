**D305 (LOCKED Session 397)** — The claim flow gets a Zelbi checkout screen; the native wallet stops being a takeover. SUPERSEDES D303's wallet-first structure. Nick's ruling: leading with Apple Pay is cleaner for the majority of iPhone users but strands everyone else, and recovering with a prompt after a wallet dismissal is the wrong shape — both routes belong on screen at once. The claim button will open a Zelbi-owned screen carrying the item, the amount, the Zelbi mark, the fee disclosure, and the payment methods including Apple Pay, with Stripe owning only the fields it must own. Three options were weighed and two rejected on established facts read from Stripe's documentation and the installed SDK this session: theming the existing sheet through the Appearance API was rejected because it accepts fonts, colours, corner radius, border width, shadow and primary-button styling but no image or logo of any kind, and Stripe states the layout stays fixed; the embedded Payment Element was rejected as the entry point because its types export in the pinned 0.50.3 but whether a callable component ships with them is unconfirmed. FlowController is the chosen route — Stripe's sheet collects the method, the app confirms on its own screen, and `PaymentOption` hands back the chosen method's label and brand icon for the app to render. This build is now the home of launch-sequence gate g1: the current sheet says "Pay $2.00", which is false, under one generic consent line that names neither the hold nor the automatic capture, and no Zelbi-owned surface exists for a correct disclosure to sit on. ORDER IS BINDING: mockup first, then the disclosure wording written into the fee and refund policy per the six-point controlling list in docs/agents/zelbi-document-requirements.md section 4.1, then the screen built to match — never the screen first. NOT YET ESTABLISHED: whether `FlowController` is exported by the pinned SDK; `grep` of the SDK index returned `CustomerSheet` and not `FlowController`, which is a flag rather than a finding since it may be exposed through the `useStripe` hook. The `applePay` parameter shipped at `975f0c3` stays and becomes load-bearing rather than optional. Origin note: Nick first asked for this redesign on 2026-06-27 and it was never filed anywhere, surviving only in that chat until it was recovered by search this session.
